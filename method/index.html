How the rating works

No black boxes. Ratings come from the Glicko system — the same approach behind competitive chess and table-tennis rankings — driven purely by your recorded duels.

The idea in 3 steps

01
Everyone starts from their old rating. The original 0–100 scores are converted into Glicko starting points (old 80 ≈ 1500), so history carries over.
02
Every match moves the numbers. Beat someone stronger and you gain more; lose to someone weaker and you drop more. Results are processed in 30-day rating periods.
03
Your rating comes with a certainty score. Each player carries an RD (ratings deviation). More reliable match data = lower RD. The leaderboard ranks visible rating = Glicko − 0.5 × RD, so uncertain ratings are held back until they've earned trust.
Calibration safeguard

Provisional influence

New players start at 1500 Glicko / 250 RD. Until they have 3 rated matches against 2 different opponents, an established opponent's rating change is scaled to 25%. The newcomer's own rating still moves at 100%.

It works in both directions — a newcomer's win or loss can only move an established player by a quarter of the usual amount, so one unknown result cannot swing the board.

An 1800 who loses to an uncalibrated newcomer takes a quarter of the usual hit; the newcomer still climbs — or falls — at full speed. Once calibrated, every result counts in full.

What is Glicko?

Glicko is a rating system that works like Elo, but smarter: every opponent is weighted by how reliable their rating is, and your own reliability is tracked at the same time. Beat someone stronger and you gain more; lose to someone weaker and you drop more.

visible = glicko − 0.5 × RD   ·   RD shrinks with results, grows with inactivity

What is RD?

RD shows how certain the system is about your rating. More reliable match data = lower RD. The leaderboard ranks the visible rating — Glicko minus a small uncertainty penalty — so an unproven rating can't rocket to the top on luck alone.

How you get ranked

Play at least 5 matches against at least 3 different opponents. Until then you're provisional: your rating is real and takes part in every calculation — it just isn't ranked until it's earned.

How inactivity affects you

Play regularly and your certainty stays high. Stop playing and your RD grows a little every 30 days — which slowly pulls your visible rating down until you return. After a full year without a dated match you're marked inactive and hidden from the board (still searchable, still in the maths) until you play again.

Advanced — the maths & live settings seeds, formulas, periods, verification

Step 1 · Starting ratings (seeds)

Players from the old 0–100 ladder keep their history: the old score is converted into a starting Glicko with the midpoint mapping below. A player with no old rating starts unrated with maximum uncertainty. Very low old scores never start below the floor the master sheet uses.

seed glicko = max( 700 , 1500 + (old − 80) × 30 )
seed RD = 80  ·  unrated start = 1500 / RD 250

The floor matters: an old score of 8 maps to −660 by the raw formula, but the master sheet seeds that player at 700 — and this site reproduces it exactly.

Step 2 · The Glicko update

Glicko is Elo's smarter cousin: every opponent is weighted by how certain their rating is, and each batch of results shrinks your own RD. With q = ln(10)/400 and:

g(RD) = 1 / √(1 + 3·q²·RD²/π²)
E(r, ro) = 1 / (1 + 10^(−g(RDo)·(r − ro)/400))
D² = 1 / (q²·Σ g²·E·(1−E))
r′ = r + (q/(1/RD² + q²·Σg²E(1−E)))·Σ g·(s − E)
RD′ = √(1 / (1/RD² + q²·Σg²E(1−E)))

s is the match score (1 win, 0 loss, ½ draw). All games inside one rating period are processed simultaneously from the same snapshot — exactly like the official sheet.

Step 3 · Rating periods & legacy matches

Dated matches are grouped into chronological 30-day rating periods. Old archive matches whose dates were never recorded — legacy matches — are processed together as one simultaneous period at the legacy grace start, so unknown dates can't be gamed.

period = ⌊date / 30 days⌋  ·  legacy = one simultaneous batch

The inactivity automation

Ratings decay automatically when you stop playing — the same behaviour as the master sheet's auto-recalculation:

  • ↓
    RD grows every 30 days of silence. Each full rating period without a match adds inactivity growth inside the square root — RD′ = √(RD² + 20²·k) for k silent periods, capped at 250. Because visible rating subtracts 0.5 × RD, an inactive player's visible rating sinks on its own.
  • 365
    Hidden after 365 days. A qualified player who hasn't logged a dated match in 365 days is hidden from the leaderboard (still searchable, still in the maths) until they return.
  • L
    Legacy grace. Date-unknown veterans stay visible for 365 days from the grace start (2026-10-04) before the inactivity rule applies to them.

Every stat, explained

  • G
    Glicko — the raw model output (the true estimated skill).
  • RD
    Ratings deviation — the uncertainty. Shrinks with results (never above 250, never below… well, very confident), grows with silence. RD ~50 = well known; RD 200+ = brand new.
  • V
    Visible rating — what the board ranks by: Glicko − 0.5 × RD. Uncertainty is held back until trust is earned.
  • %
    Win % — wins / matches, rounded at the display precision.
  • Ø
    Avg opp — the average final Glicko of your distinct opponents. High here means you've been fighting the top of the board.

The rules, at a glance

  • +
    A win over a stronger opponent gains more. A loss to a weaker one costs more.
  • ↓
    Inactivity raises uncertainty. Visible rating subtracts half of RD, so a silent rating gradually sinks.
