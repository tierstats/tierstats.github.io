How the rating works

No black boxes. The system is Dynamic Glicko-2 — the same maths behind competitive chess and table-tennis rankings, tuned for 1v1 tank duels.

The idea in 3 steps

  • 1
    Everyone starts from their old rating. The original 0–100 scores are converted into Glicko starting points (old 80 ≈ 1500), so history carries over.
  • 2
    Every match moves the numbers. Beat someone stronger and you gain more; lose to someone weaker and you drop more. Results are processed in 30-day rating periods.
  • 3
    Your rating knows what it doesn't know. Each player carries an RD (ratings deviation) — a certainty score. The leaderboard shows visible rating = Glicko − 0.35 × RD, so uncertain ratings are held back until they've earned trust.
visible = glicko − 0.35 × RD   ·   RD shrinks with results, grows with inactivity

Who appears on the board?

A player is ranked once they have at least 5 matches against at least 3 different opponents. Players whose latest match is over 365 days old are marked inactive — their rating is frozen until they return. Provisional players are still climbing to (or falling from) their true level.
